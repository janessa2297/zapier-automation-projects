Why Keeping the Commandments Starts with Love, Not Fear


## Generated Blog Content


When your love for God feels like it is running dry, it is easy to assume you just need to try harder. However, the issue is rarely a lack of effort; rather, it is a failure to remember. 

In Deuteronomy chapter 11, the text commands believers to love the Lord and keep His charge and commandments always. This often raises a difficult question: Do you always keep the commandments? If you did, you probably wouldn't sin. 

### The Root of Obedience: Love Versus Fear

Many people mistakenly believe they must keep the commandments to earn salvation or to prove their love to God. In reality, that mindset is completely backwards. We do not keep commandments to *get* God's love; we keep them *because* we love God first. 

When people operate under the belief that obedience creates love, fear is usually at the center. The underlying mindset becomes, "If I break this law, I am going to hell." That is self-serving and rooted in fear, not genuine love. 

The Christian life often feels like a roller coaster—up and down, happy and sad, marked by cycles of sin and repentance. God does not demand that we grit our teeth, want it more, or stop backsliding through sheer human willpower. Instead, He points us back to the source: His love. As 1 John 4:19 reminds us, "We love him because he loved us first."

### The Law Reveals Sin, It Cannot Heal Sin

The law acts as a mirror; it reveals our sin, but it cannot heal it. When you try to keep the law through your own strength, you inevitably realize you cannot do it. You realize that you fall short. 

Yet, even while we fall short and fail to love Him perfectly, Christ still died on the cross for us. When you recognize that God still blesses you—providing family, food, and life despite your shortcomings—real agape love begins to arise in your heart. 

This is the beauty of substitution. You cannot save yourself, which is why Jesus had to do it. You cannot die for your sins without facing eternal separation, which is why He died in your place. When your love runs dry, you haven't stopped trying; you've stopped remembering what Christ accomplished.

### Leaving the Past Behind

Deuteronomy chapter 11 also points believers back to God's past acts, such as when He parted the Red Sea, saved Israel, and drowned the Egyptian army. Egypt is symbolic of the world and sin. Just as the Israelites could not lead themselves out of Egypt, you cannot overcome sin entirely on your own. 

Furthermore, the water that drowned Egypt represents baptism and the permanent removal of your old life. Your past sins, failures, and the version of yourself you are ashamed of were cast into the depths of the ocean. 

As Micah 7:19 promises, God subdues our iniquities and casts our sins into the depths of the sea. The only power your past has is the power you continue to give it through agreement, rumination, and dwelling on shame. When your past comes up, you must remember that God has already drowned it. 

### Grace Versus Pride

When people hear that Jesus did all the work and that salvation is by grace alone through faith, a common reaction is prideful: "Does that mean I can just sin however I want?" 

Thinking you must add your own works to Christ's finished work is a form of pride. It implies that Jesus' sacrifice on the cross was not enough. The Bible makes it clear that we are saved by grace, and our life is hidden in Christ. 

Ultimately, the Christian walk comes down to a choice of authority. If you only follow God when you agree with Him, then God is not truly your master—you are. True humility means trusting the Word of God over your own thoughts, feelings, and past trauma. Focus on the finished work of Christ, remember His unfailing love, and trust the Savior who does what you cannot do yourself.
